Newton's cradle: XPBD against impulses

Balls on rigid rods, pins one diameter apart, so neighbors just touch. Pull one back and let go: an ideal cradle swings exactly one ball out on the far side. Every contact in the row is hit in the same substep, and how a solver handles simultaneous contacts decides whether the impulse travels down the row or smears across it. Here bounces are resolved in rounds (Generalized Reflections, Smith et al. 2012), so both solvers pass it down. What's left is the rods: XPBD projects positions, and the projection takes a share of the swing that halves each time the substeps double (1.3% at 10). The impulse solver is closer at every count. There's no iterations slider: each ball hangs on its own rod, so extra iterations change nothing (measured, docs/08-collision.md). Grab a ball and pull more of them.
