Piano: stiff strings, a felt hammer

A2's string, made of steel. A stiff string is part bar, so its overtones run sharp: partial k sits at k·F₀·√(1 + Bk²), not k·f₀. On the spectrum, the gray lines are where a perfect string's harmonics would be, and the yellow ticks where this one's are: watch them drift apart up the spectrum. The strings are struck by a felt hammer that stays on them for a millisecond or a few (the force plot), and the felt stiffens as it's squeezed, so a harder strike is brighter, not only louder. Most keys have two or three strings a cent apart, beating slowly; a few body resonances color the sound.

Play with the keyboard (home row and the row above, Z / X for octaves) or the keys below: press low on a key to strike harder. The computer keyboard uses the velocity slider. Turn the stiffness off to hear the same piano with ideal strings. The first note waits a moment while every key's strings are designed.
