Waveguide string: where you pluck, where you listen

A string is two waves: one running toward the bridge, one toward the nut, each turning upside down where the string is held. Their sum is the string's shape, drawn below (the bridge at the left). Pluck it at a point and listen at a point, and the harmonics follow sin(kπβ)·sin(kπp): pluck at ½ and every even harmonic is gone (a node sits exactly where you pulled), at ⅓ every third, and the same for the pickup. Watch the spectrum: the red ticks are the harmonics the physics says should be missing. A1's Karplus–Strong loop is this same string folded up and heard at the bridge.

Play with the keyboard (home row and the row above, Z / X for octaves) or the keys below. The pluck position takes effect from the next note; drag the pickup while a note rings and hear its tone change.
