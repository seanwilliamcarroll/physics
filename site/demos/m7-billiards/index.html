Billiards: slide, roll, and why speculative contacts

A 9-ft table to the WPA's specification. Drag back from anywhere and let go to strike the cue ball: the pull sets the aim and the speed. The tip sets where the cue meets the ball: a centre hit slides, then rolls at 5/7 of its speed; struck 2/5 of a radius up it rolls at once; below centre it carries backspin. Hit an object ball full and the cloth does the rest: a stun stops dead, follow rolls on through, draw comes back. The physics is a top-down view of a 3D ball: it carries spin about horizontal axes, and the cloth acts where it touches (docs/09-billiards.md). Turn speculative contacts off and break hard at 1–2 substeps: balls pass through each other, and the tunnel counter says so. With them on, nothing tunnels at any substeps.

Energy

Kinetic energy plus spin, in joules (log scale): the cloth only takes it away, sliding fast and rolling slowly, until the table is still.
